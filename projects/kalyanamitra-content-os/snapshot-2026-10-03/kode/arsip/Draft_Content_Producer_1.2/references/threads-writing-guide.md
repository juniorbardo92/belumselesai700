# Threads writing guide

## Audience and promise

Default audience: Indonesian Buddhist young adults, generally 18–35, who want clear and practical Dharma for work pressure, relationships, emotions, digital life, and questions of direction.

Every thread should help the reader:

1. recognize one specific situation;
2. understand one sourced Dharma lens;
3. try one safe practice or reflection;
4. know where to verify or continue learning.

Do not write generic “wise” posters. Start from a concrete human situation and fulfill the hook's promise.

## Voice application

Read [voice-profile.md](voice-profile.md) before drafting. Carry its conversational, question-led, community-teaching cadence across the whole thread. Adapt the strengths of the WhatsApp source voice for public Threads: connected short steps, inclusive **kita**, one vivid analogy when useful, a sourced Dharma lens, and one memorable practice. Do not copy private-chat fragments, distinctive sentences, personal experiences, or unsupported assertions.

## Editorial mix for a monthly plan

Use these four pillars as a planning target, not a rigid per-part quota:

| Pillar | Monthly target | Typical topics |
|---|---:|---|
| Dharma untuk Kehidupan Sehari-hari | 40% | Work stress, anger, relationships, loneliness, desire, digital ethics, failure, change |
| Tiga Tradisi, Satu Topik | 25% | Compassion, meditation, karma, wisdom, bodhisattva, Nibbana/Nirvana, ritual |
| Mitos, Istilah, dan Ketepatan Sumber | 20% | False quotations, Pali/Sanskrit terms, karma, meditation myths, doctrine versus culture, source checking |
| Praktik Kecil Hari Ini | 15% | One-minute reflection, attention, virtue journal, mindful speech, compassion, family practice |

Across 30 days and 150 threads, vary the audience problem, emotional tone, teaching function, hook pattern, practice, and CTA. A new title alone does not make a thread unique.

## Thread structure

Each thread contains 6–8 posts. Use a coherent progression. A common seven-post shape is:

1. `hook`: a specific problem, tension, or misconception.
2. `context`: why it matters in ordinary life.
3. `dharma-lens`: the sourced teaching or framework.
4. `explanation`: clarify meaning and prevent a common misunderstanding.
5. `application`: connect the lens to a realistic situation.
6. `practice` or `reflection`: one safe action or precise question.
7. `summary-source-cta`: concise recap, source direction, and optional ethical CTA.

Other structures are allowed when the roles remain clear and the thread develops rather than repeats itself.

For the Ruang Kalyanamitra voice, a useful eight-post variation is: recognizable situation, diagnostic question, ordinary reaction, story or analogy, sourced Dharma lens, clarification, practice, and reflective close. Combine adjacent roles for a six- or seven-post thread.

## Post rules

- Maximum 500 Unicode characters per post.
- Prefer one function and one main thought per post.
- Post 1 must stand alone as a truthful hook.
- Explain unfamiliar Pali, Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, or Japanese terms in Indonesian.
- Put exact source details in the source records. A brief reader-facing source note may appear in the final post.
- Use no more than one CTA, only in the final post.
- Do not repeat “DRAF”, source disclaimers, or CTA in every post; those belong in package metadata or the final post.
- Do not label public copy as “Hari 1”, “Hari 2”, and so forth unless the user explicitly requests a public numbered series.
- Avoid excessive emojis, slogans, hashtags, capital letters, and engagement bait.
- Do not ask readers to share merely to prove identity, goodness, faith, or loyalty.

## Hook patterns

Vary truthful hook patterns:

- recognizable moment: “Pernah menutup chat, tetapi percakapannya terus berputar di kepala?”
- misconception: “Meditasi bukan tombol untuk mematikan pikiran.”
- consequence: “Kemarahan lima menit bisa tinggal dalam tubuh sepanjang hari.”
- contrast: “Diam belum tentu tenang; berbicara belum tentu tidak sadar.”
- precise question: “Apa yang sebenarnya kita lindungi ketika sulit meminta maaf?”
- source-checking: “Kutipan ini sering disebut sabda Buddha. Benarkah?”

Do not use false urgency, guaranteed outcomes, invented controversy, or attacks on people and traditions.

## “Tiga Tradisi, Satu Topik”

For cross-tradition threads:

- export `tradition_scope=cross-tradition`;
- list every tradition actually covered;
- label each perspective in the relevant post;
- attach source IDs to the claims for that perspective;
- state similarities carefully and preserve meaningful differences;
- avoid a conclusion that declares a winner.

## CTA options

Use a CTA only when it follows naturally from the thread:

- save the thread for a specific future reflection;
- read the exact source listed;
- try the stated practice once and observe carefully;
- answer one precise, non-performative reflection question;
- send it privately to one person who may genuinely benefit.

## Monthly duplicate check

Before export, compare every draft against all completed parts for:

- central claim;
- opening hook;
- story or scenario;
- teaching and source combination;
- practice;
- closing question;
- CTA wording.

If prior parts are unavailable, do not claim cross-part checking passed. Add `cross-part-duplicate-check-pending` to the package risk flags.

## Quality threshold

A thread is ready for import as a draft only when it is relevant, clear, practical, source-aware, ethically shareable, and distinct from the rest of the package. “Ready for import” does not mean “ready to publish”.
